Theoretical basis: The Community of Inquiry theoretical framework was developed by D. Randy Garrison, Terry Anderson and Walter Archer through a Canadian Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council-funded study of text-based computer conferencing conducted from 1997 to 2001. The theoretical foundation was set out in their 2000 paper, Critical Inquiry in a Text-Based Environment: Computer Conferencing in Higher Education. Read the CoI website’s explanation of the framework.

About this tool: The CoI Praxis Framework presented on this site was developed from research conducted by Sam Cogan with participatory action research (PAR) participants from National College of Ireland, as part of an EdD at the University of Sheffield. It applies the established CoI theoretical framework by bringing together approaches identified in the literature and through the PAR process. This tool is a practice-focused outcome of that research; it is not part of, or an official product of, the original Community of Inquiry framework.

The survey and the Praxis Framework: The survey provides a starting point for using the Praxis Framework. The questions come from the original learner-facing Community of Inquiry Survey, developed and validated by a collaborative research team. They are reframed here as an educator reflective tool: educators place themselves in their learners’ shoes and consider how learners might respond. The resulting scores are prompts for reflection, not an evaluation or diagnosis. Each survey category connects to the corresponding part of the Praxis Framework, where users can examine relevant approaches identified through the research.
